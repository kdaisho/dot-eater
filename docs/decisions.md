# Decisions

Settled decisions, grouped by topic. "Product decision" or "with the user" means the user decided: don't reopen it without asking. The handover (`docs/handover.md`) keeps only the decisions the next work touches.

## Gameplay and characters

- **Core** (product decisions): grid-based movement with smooth animation (not free movement); each character has a signature breath plus one stat; breaths use an energy meter refilled by eating dots; local 2-player has both co-op and versus; stages unlock in order.
- **Characters** (with the user): five animals in `roster.ts` (Dragon, Penguin, Owl, Skunk, Boar). The Dragon 🐉 is the default; the fly 🪰 is retired. The player sprite is the playing character's emoji. Stars and par are the same for every character (revisit after playtesting).
- **Score:** `DOT_POINTS` 10.
- **Health** (KDA-257, with the user; the numbers are placeholders to tune at preview): there are no lives. Each player has its own `Player.health`, `MAX_HEALTH` 100, full at the start of every stage. An enemy's touch costs `ENEMY_DAMAGE` 25, the boss's `BOSS_DAMAGE` 40 (the boss first when both touch on one tick, so only the harder hit counts). `hurtPlayer` in `game.ts` is the one path every hit takes (the boss's breath attack calls it too, see Enemies → Boss). A hit resets nobody: enemies, the boss, dots, tile effects, energy and the cooldown stay as they are.
    - **Knockback:** a hit with health left knocks the player one cell away from what hit it, along the axis it is further off on (x on a tie; from the enemy's drawn position, the boss's footprint centre; drawn right on it, it backs away from its facing). `knockBack(state, player, step)` is general (KDA-258's punch will use it): the cell is one on from the cell the player is drawn nearest; a wall there (or anything else that blocks a normal move) and it stays put. It glides there like a normal move at its normal speed, eats a dot there and slides on ice, but keeps its facing and tap buffer; when the new cell lies on the glide it is in, the glide carries on from where it is drawn, else it starts from that nearest cell, at most half a cell off. Less than halfway out of a cell with no way on past it, it turns back into that cell from where it is drawn, so it never glides on into what hit it. A knockback that eats the last dot wins on that tick (clearing is checked again after the hits).
    - **Invulnerability:** after a hit the player is invulnerable for `HURT_INVULNERABLE_SECONDS` 1.5 (counted in ticks, `Player.invulnerableFor`): enemies and the boss pass through it, and it blinks (`playerVisible`, `BLINK_RATE`, starting on).
    - **Knockout** (health 0): solo, the stage is failed (the usual game over, no knockback); co-op and versus, see Two players. Eating the last dot always wins, even if hit on that tick, which then costs nothing (product decision; in versus a knockout on that tick still costs its dots).
- **Stars and bests:** 1 star for clearing, +1 for clearing with at least half health (`MAX_HEALTH / 2`, the solo player's health at the clear; KDA-257), +1 for a clear time at or under the level's `parTime` (par rounded to whole ticks). Clear time is `GameState.ticks`, counted only by `step()`, so pauses and the ending outro don't count; shown as `m:ss.s`, rounded up. Bests are per stage and per difficulty in the v1 save (see Progress), each `stars` (max) and `bestTime` (min, ticks); merging with another tab keeps the better of each. Par times: stage 1 80 s (medium map) and 140 s (large; KDA-273); stage 2 95 s and 150 s (KDA-274); stage 3 115 s and 200 s (KDA-275); stage 4 115 s and 192 s (KDA-276); stage 5 60 s and 75 s (KDA-277); stage 6 75 s and 110 s (KDA-278); stage 7 80 s and 125 s (KDA-279); stage 8 110 s and 220 s (KDA-280); stage 9 100 s and 180 s (KDA-281). A remade map's par is about 1.37 times a nearest-dot route over its dots at 4 cells/s, rounded: the margin the 17x15 maps had (stages 6 and 7 a little more, for their enemies). Stage 3's is about 1.33 times the route of a player that slides, the old ice rink's margin, as slides make the route longer. Stage 4's is about 1.73 times, the old fog maze's margin, as the player can't see where the dots are. Stage 8's (ice and fog) is about 1.6 times the route of a player that slides. Stage 9's is about 1.45 times, for its enemies. A boss stage's par doesn't follow the dot-route rule, as it is won by defeating the boss, not by eating the dots: it is about twice the time a seeded bot (one that aims, dodges the breath and reloads at the nearest dots) takes to defeat the boss, for a player who misses and has to reload more (stage 5's bot: 23–40 s).

## Two players (KDA-211)

- **State:** `GameState.players` holds 1 or 2 `Player`s (`character`, `breather`, `bufferedStep`, `score`, `eatenAt`, `rate`); `step(state, dt, moves, random)` takes one `MoveInput` per player (a single one is player 1's, the rest idle). Players move in order (player 1 eats a dot both reach on one tick), pass through each other (unless they bump, see Bump) and each breathe their own breath; breaths don't affect players. The game's score is `totalScore`; breath kills and the boss score for the player who breathed.
- **Targeting** (with the user): every enemy goes after the nearest player by maze distance from where it stands, over the cells as enemies see them, so a player a blocking tile cuts off counts as unreachable (`nearestTarget`; plain maze distance if neither can be reached; a phantom measures Manhattan, through walls); the boss the player it can get nearest, where its 2x2 footprint fits, then the one nearest its footprint by path now (`bossTarget`), so a player in a corridor it can't enter doesn't draw it from one it can reach. Ties go to player 1. With one player nothing changed.
- **Hits and knockouts:** `GameState.mode` (from `createGameState(level, animals, mode)`, which `startLevel` passes) picks the rules; `hurtPlayer` applies a hit and `knockOut` in `game.ts` is the one place that decides what a knockout costs per mode. Each player has its own health (see Health). Solo: a knockout loses the game. Eating the last dot wins whichever player eats it (on a boss level, defeating the boss wins instead, whichever player does it; KDA-267), even with a player out.
- **Co-op knockouts** (KDA-221, KDA-257, with the user): each player has its own health gauge (no shared pool). A player knocked out is taken out alone, for good (`takeOut` with `respawnIn: null`): not drawn, no moving, breathing, eating or being hurt, enemies and the boss ignore it, its input is ignored. Enemies, the boss, dots and the other player are not reset. Two hit on one tick are hurt in player order. The game is lost once no player is in play (`outForGood` for all). Taking out with a wait and `bringBack` (back at its own spawn after `PLAYER_RESPAWN_DELAY`, 3 s counted in ticks, ready to move, facing right, nothing buffered, full health, energy and score kept) are now versus's. While nobody is in play, every enemy and the boss head back to their own spawns (random enemies and patrollers keep their own ways), so none parks on a waiting player's spawn; once a player is back, they go after the players again. Fog lights stay where the players went out. In the HUD an out player's meter slot reads "back in 3/2/1", "back soon" while it waits for its spawn, or "OUT". Breaths never affect the partner (each breath tested in `game.coop.test.ts`). The 2-player pause screen lists both players' resume keys (1P P/Esc/Space, 2P Q/E).
- **Respawning onto an occupied spawn** (PM decision; versus since KDA-257): a player that is due waits (at `respawnIn` 0) while any enemy in play or the boss is drawn within `RESPAWN_CLEAR_DISTANCE` (1.5 cells; the boss by its footprint's square) of its spawn, or is in a move that ends on the spawn or next to it (harmless enemies and a defeated boss don't count), and is back on the first tick it is clear. So nothing gliding in can catch it on the tick it is back or the next (checking only for a catch at once let an enemy 0.65 cells off catch it a tick later). No stage has an enemy or boss spawn within `RESPAWN_CLEAR_DISTANCE + 1` of a player spawn (tested), so enemies gone home never hold a spawn up.
- **Versus** (KDA-222, with the user): two players race for the dots on the same map; pure rules in `versus.ts`. Offered on the title as the third mode (`MODE_CHOICES`).
    - **Stuns:** a breath stuns the other player for `STUN_SECONDS` (2 s) instead of defeating it. One rule for every breath: the other player (in play) standing, by its cell, on any cell the breath strikes (the cells enemies would be hit on: the shape's cells plus the origin for a line or a cone) is stunned (`opponentsStruck`, called from `breathe`). So the Dragon stuns along its whole line even past an enemy it defeats, the Owl doesn't push the player, and the Boar's headbutt stuns a player in the cell ahead. A breath that stuns counts as fired even when its hook refused (the Boar with no wall to break), so it pays its normal cost and cooldown. Only the strike stuns: lasting tiles (fire, stink, ice) never do. A stunned player can't be stunned again until it is over (the timer doesn't reset), and once it wears off it is immune for `STUN_IMMUNITY_SECONDS` (1.5 s, with the user), so a breath fired whenever it is free can't keep it stunned; being taken out ends the immunity, as it ends a stun (our call). Stunned: its input is ignored, it finishes the glide it is in and, on ice, its slide (PM decision: only voluntary moves and breaths are blocked), and doesn't move on or breathe; it can still be caught, and going out ends the stun. Player 1's breath fires first, so on a tick both breathe at each other, player 1's stun stops player 2's breath. Never in co-op or solo.
    - **Knockouts** (KDA-257): hits hurt as in every mode (see Health). A player knocked out loses `CATCH_DOTS` (5) dots, not below 0, keeps its score, and is out for `PLAYER_RESPAWN_DELAY` (3 s), then back at its spawn with full health (`knockOut`, `bringBack`). A player eats only by moving onto a cell, so `bringBack` eats a dot on the cell it lands on, if one is there (KDA-270, a safety net: nothing should put one on a spawn, but the nearest free cell of a held-up spawn may hold one). A knockout on the tick the last dot is eaten (or, on a boss stage, the boss is defeated) still costs its dots and takes the player out before the round is scored (with the user; solo and co-op keep "the last dot always wins", free). A versus game is never lost.
    - **Dots and score:** `Player.dots` counts dots eaten, less knockout penalties, and decides rounds; score still counts dots and breath kills (the boss's +500 too, which adds no dots).
    - **Match:** best of 3 on one stage. A round ends when the stage is cleared (all dots gone, or on a boss stage the boss defeated, with dots left or not: KDA-267). Winner: most dots (also on a boss stage, where the player who defeats the boss gets its +500 score but no dots), then higher score; an exact tie is replayed and doesn't count (`roundWinner`). First to `ROUNDS_TO_WIN` (2) takes the match (`matchWinner`). Versus game screens carry `match` (the rounds so far); a round goes to `roundResult` (winner, dots and score per player, tally, and a Next round / Quit menu, `ROUND_CHOICES`: Next round plays on a fresh copy of the stage, same characters; Quit drops the match for stage select, still in versus, with the user) and the deciding round to `matchResult` (winner, a line per round, Rematch / Stage select). Both keep the `END_CONFIRM_DELAY` confirm lock (the cursor moves meanwhile) and follow player 1. Pause stays resume-only. Versus never reaches stage clear or game over and never touches saved progress or the last pick.
    - **HUD:** per player its dot count (`1P ●12`) in place of the score, and a stun countdown (`💫 2`) or respawn countdown in place of its meter (its health gauge stays under a stun countdown); the round and tally (`Round 2 · 1–0`) in the middle. A stunned player's sprite wears a 💫 badge, and an immune one a 🛡️ badge. The HUD under a result screen still shows the round as it was played.
- **Modes:** the title is the mode select; `Mode` is `"solo" | "coop" | "versus"` and every screen after the title carries it. `MODE_CHOICES` in `flow.ts` lists what the title offers, in order: `solo` ("1 player"), `coop` ("Co-op") and `versus` ("Versus"). Labels are `MODE_LABELS` in `main.ts`. 1 player and co-op then pick story or stage mode (KDA-271, see Story mode); versus is stage only (PM call), so it goes straight to stage select (`hasStory`).
- **Character select (2 players):** each player moves its own cursor and confirms (`ready`); the game starts once both have. Both may pick the same character. Back from a confirmed player takes the confirmation back, otherwise it goes to difficulty select. Menus other than character select (difficulty select included) follow player 1; during a 2-player game either player pauses and resumes.
- **Gamepads** (with the user): in 1-player every pad drives player 1 (`"shared"`); in a 2-player mode pads take players in connection order, pad 1 player 1 and pad 2 player 2 (`"perPad"`). `padAssignment` in `main.ts` switches on each screen change: the title always shares, so any pad picks the mode; from stage select on the mode decides, so player 2's pad picks its character.
- **Progress** (with the user): 2-player games don't record clears, bests or stars (`recordsProgress`) or the last character or difficulty pick, and can play any stage unlocked in 1-player. A co-op story run (KDA-271, PM call) continues from the 1-player furthest stage and records nothing either: no unlocks, bests or story best. They pick a difficulty like 1-player (difficulty select opens on the saved 1-player pick), but never save it.
- **Looks:** with two players each sprite gets a ring and its number in `PLAYER_COLORS` (blue, red); the HUD shows each player's score, meter and health gauge (under the meter) in those colours; the end screens (stage clear and game over) show the combined score, then a line per player with its share and the dots it ate (`Player.dots`). Fog lights up round both players.
- **Bump** (KDA-262): Settings → Players, **Pass through** (default) or **Bump** (`settings.playersBump`), in co-op and versus; with one player it changes nothing. The game reads `GameState.playersBump`, which `createGameState` leaves off and flow sets from the saved settings on every playing tick, so a change made from pause applies once play resumes. Bumping, a player can't move into the other player's logical cell (`pos`: the cell it is on, or moving into; `playerBlocks` in `game.ts`), so two players next to each other can't swap. It waits there as at a wall: ready at exactly 1, facing the way it pushed, and its held direction is tried again on the next tick (a buffered tap is used up at that opportunity, as at a wall). The cell the other is gliding out of doesn't count, so a player can follow right behind the other (blocking it too would stop a follower for good, since the leader always moves on before its glide ends). Following doesn't depend on the player order: a player whose first way (its slide step, else the buffered tap, then the held directions; walls skipped) is blocked only by a player still to move this tick moves after the others instead (`defersMove`), with nothing tried or used up before, so it goes straight on behind the other rather than down an older held direction. Two heading into one empty cell on a tick: player 1 gets it and player 2 waits (player order). A wall growing back (the Boar's) doesn't push a player onto the other: it goes to the nearest open cell free of the other player instead. A slide stops next to the other player as at a wall (and carries on if the other moves off before the slider makes a move of its own, since the slide is worked out from where it stands and came from). A player out of play never blocks. A player due back waits (at `respawnIn` 0, like the enemy rule above) while the other is on its spawn or moving onto it, and is back on the first tick it isn't. **Spawn camping** (PM call): once the other player has held it up for `RESPAWN_BLOCK_GRACE` (2 s of such ticks, `Player.spawnHeldFor`), it comes back on the nearest free cell to its spawn instead (breadth-first along open cells, direction order on ties; `nearestFreeCell`): not the other player's cell, and clear of enemies and the boss by the respawn-clear rule above, so never onto or right next to an enemy. Waits an enemy or the boss causes don't count towards it and stay as before. A knockback (KDA-257) never puts a player on the other's cell either: with Bump on, that cell is a wall to `knockBack`. Players never block enemies or the boss, and enemies never block players.

## Breaths

- **Energy** (KDA-208, with the user): the meter holds 100 (Skunk 150) and gains 5 per dot; it starts empty each stage and is kept when the player is hurt. Costs: Dragon 50, Penguin 50, Owl 25, Skunk 50, Boar 75; no breath without enough energy. Cooldown 1 s after each breath (Owl 0.5 s). Breath kills score 50. A press that can't breathe is dropped, not buffered. A breath that refuses (`fire` returns `false`) spends nothing and starts no cooldown.
- **Defeated enemies** (with the user): respawn alone at their spawn after 5 s, blinking for the last 1 s; while gone they don't move, hurt or show. A hit on the player resets none of them (KDA-257: the old "a lost life sends every enemy, the boss and the Splitter back to their spawns" is gone). An enemy coming back onto a player standing on its spawn can hurt it at once (the blink is the warning).
- **Facing** (with the user: starts right, breathing mid-slide allowed): explicit on the player (`Breather.facing`), the way it last moved, including slide steps and tunnels. At a move opportunity where every tried way is blocked it turns to the first one tried, so it can face a wall. A knockback doesn't turn it; it faces right again when a versus player comes back. A mid-slide breath fires along the slide.
- **Statuses:** a held enemy stays where it is drawn (drawn without frame alpha, and under the players, so one standing on it shows; other enemies are drawn over the players), a harmless one doesn't hurt, and one with `blinksBeforeEnd` blinks for the status's last 1 s. No status blocks the player: players walk onto and through every enemy's cell, a frozen one included (KDA-260 dropped the `solid` flag, the Penguin's freeze its only user). An enemy never ends a slide itself, but the floor under it may: a frozen enemy on level ice is slid over, while one in the Penguin's own breath ice stands on a cell left as floor (breath ice never forms under a character), so a slide ends on its cell. An enemy that stops being harmless within catching distance of a player hurts it as usual, e.g. a thawing one the player stands on (unchanged). Enemies still overlap each other.
- **Changing grids:** `GameState.ice` is the level's ice (`floorIce`) plus ice tile effects and is replaced, never mutated, when it changes, so `hasIce`'s per-grid cache holds. Opened walls change `cells` directly. Temporary ice never forms under a character.
- **The five breaths** (KDA-216–220; the numbers are tunable after playtesting):
    - 🐉 Dragon: line, range 5. Defeats the first enemy hit (an enemy on the player's own cell counts first) and stops there; the cells it passed burn 2 s (`dragon.burning`, blocks enemies, not the player). Refuses facing a wall with nothing to hit.
    - 🐧 Penguin: cone, range 3. Freezes enemies 3 s (holds, harmless; players walk through them; blinks for the last 1 s as a thaw warning, `blinksBeforeEnd`); covered floor becomes ice for 5 s. Refuses facing straight into a wall with no enemy on its cell. Stat (KDA-259, replacing "slides faster on ice"): "Walks on ice". On level and breath ice (its own or another player's) the Penguin never slides: it moves cell by cell by its input and stops when input stops, tap buffer included, as on normal floor (`BreathDef.walksOnIce`, checked in `slideTick`). Every other animal and every enemy (Phantoms aside, which never slide) still slides. `Player.rate` (the glide factor the renderer reads) stays, at 1 for every move.
    - 🦉 Owl: line, range 5. Pushes every enemy in it, one on the player's cell included, 3 cells along the facing; a 0.25 s look-only gust. Refuses when the line is empty and nobody is on the player's cell.
    - 🦨 Skunk: area, radius 1 (3×3 through open cells, the player's cell included), 5 s where breathed; blocks enemies. The 💨 centre is its own effect (`skunk.stink.centre`). An enemy already on the player's cell still catches.
    - 🐗 Boar: line, range 1. Opens the inner wall in front for 10 s (rubble look, no dot); refuses outer (border) walls and empty floor. Moves at 3.5 cells/s (`speedScale` 0.875).
- **Punch** (KDA-258, with the user; numbers are placeholders to tune at preview): every character has a quick boxing jab, a shove rather than a kill (`punch.ts`). The `punch` action (`MoveInput.punch`, a fresh press) punches on every press, one per tick at most: no energy, no cooldown, and it never stops or slows the player's movement. A player out of play or stunned (versus) can't punch.
    - **Reach:** along `Breather.facing`, from the player's drawn position (`drawnPos`, no frame alpha, like collisions): anything drawn more than 0 and up to `PUNCH_REACH` ahead (a third of a sprite past the player's front to the target's front: `SPRITE_SCALE / 2 + SPRITE_SCALE / 3 + SPRITE_SCALE / 2`, about 1.07 cells, so an enemy resting on the next cell is in reach) and within `PUNCH_ACROSS` (0.5 cell) of the facing line, wrap-aware (`wrapDelta`). Behind never counts.
    - **Enemies:** every enemy in play in reach is hit (our call: enemies overlap, and a jab that left one of two stacked enemies standing wouldn't clear the way), reported nearest first. Each is knocked back a cell (the first in a row; see Knockback decay) along the facing from where it is drawn (`knockEnemy`, not the breaths' `push`, which starts from the enemy's cell and could make it jump): to the cell one on from the cell it is drawn nearest, unless that is a wall. It glides there from exactly where it is drawn, with no jump (its move progress is how far ahead of that nearest cell it is drawn, so it may start below 0, down to -0.5, and glide in up to 1.5 cells). So an enemy gliding away carries on to its cell, one gliding in turns round, and spamming the punch doesn't throw it further until it is drawn nearer the next cell. An enemy gliding across the punch can't glide on a slant: it snaps across onto its nearest cell (at most half a cell) and is knocked from there (our call). It goes through tunnels and slides on if it lands on ice. It is harmless for the rest of the punch's tick (a look-less `punch.knocked` status), so the knock never catches anyone on that tick. It is never defeated, and a Splitter (whole or a half) doesn't split. A held (frozen) enemy is hit (it gets the hit's sound and burst) but not moved; a harmless one is knocked.
    - **Knockback decay** (with the user, KDA-288; the numbers are placeholders to tune at preview): tracked per enemy (`Character.knocks`, `sinceKnock`). Each landed punch knocks it back less than the last, by `KNOCK_STEPS` in `punch.ts`: 1, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3, then 0 cells for every hit after. After a lull of `KNOCK_LULL` (2 s) with no landed punch it recovers a step, then another every `KNOCK_RECOVER` (0.5 s), back to a full cell (so from 0, full again 3.5 s after the last landed punch; our call that the first step comes at 2 s, not 2.5). A short knock goes towards the same cell as a full one (one on from the cell it is drawn nearest) and glides out from where the enemy is drawn, with no jump, but stops that far past the nearest cell: it turns round there (`Character.knockTurn`, `turnKnock`) and glides back into the nearest cell. One already drawn that far out isn't moved. Walls, tunnels and the punch's-tick harmlessness are as for a full knock; a short knock never reaches the far cell, so it never slides on ice there (back on an icy nearest cell, it slides on towards the puncher, the way it came in). A 0 knock is still a hit (sound and burst) but moves nothing and isn't harmless. A knock stopped by a wall or one that moves nothing still counts towards the decay; a hit on a held enemy doesn't (our call: it is unchanged). A defeated enemy comes back fresh, and so does each half of a split Splitter. The goal: spamming one enemy stops working, so it closes in and can hurt the player, while spaced-out punches keep working.
    - **Pressing on** (with the user, KDA-288): a knocked enemy comes straight back at the player. After a full knock its previous cell is the one towards the player, which the no-reverse rule would forbid, so it may turn back into it on its next move of its own (`Character.turnsBack`, `candidateMoves` in `ai.ts`); ice slides in between don't use that up (our call, so one knocked onto ice can come back up it). A short knock needs nothing: it glides back into its nearest cell, so its previous cell is the far one. A breath's push, a defeat, a split and a regrowing wall end a knock under way. A short knock doesn't turn round into a cell a wall has grown back on, or where turning would stack it on a twin by the spread rule (`stacksOn`); it carries on into the far cell as a full knock would, free to turn back on its next move (`skipKnockTurn`; PR #98 review). The spread rule (`stacksOn`, `spreadChoice`, `mayHold`, `turnOrder`) works on positions only, so it keeps twins apart as before, a knocked one turning back included.
    - **Guard** (with the user, KDA-288; placeholders to tune at preview; `guard.ts`): each enemy rolls a guard skill as it comes into play (its first tick in play, again after each defeat; a Splitter's halves each roll their own), by the game's difficulty (`GUARD` in `difficulty.ts`, one table: easy 25% guarders with a skill of 0.2–0.5, medium 40% / 0.3–0.7, hard 60% / 0.4–0.85, super hard 75% / 0.5–0.85, evenly, capped at `GUARD_CAP` 0.85). Any enemy type can be a guarder. The rolls draw on the game's injected random source, one draw per enemy (the draw under the chance, scaled, is the skill), in slot order, after the timers and before the AI's draws. A game made without a difficulty (`createGameState`'s fourth argument; flow always passes it) has `guard: null`, rolls nothing and draws exactly as before. A punch is blocked with a chance of skill × f(interval), where the interval is the game time since that player's previous punch (`Player.sincePunch`, Infinity before the first, so the first counts as slow): f is 1 at 0.2 s or less (`QUICK_PUNCH`), 0.25 at 0.6 s or more (`SLOW_PUNCH`, `SLOW_GUARD`), linear in between. One draw per enemy in reach with a skill. A blocked punch doesn't move the enemy (nor make it harmless), doesn't count towards its decay or restart its lull, and reports `punchBlocked` in place of that enemy's `punchHit`. A held (frozen) enemy, the boss and the other player in versus never block and draw nothing.
    - **Boss:** hit when any of its body (the square of its four cell centres) is in reach, but not hurt or moved; the hit is reported (`target: "boss"`), for its thud.
    - **Players:** in co-op the punch passes through the partner, with no effect. In versus it knocks the other player in play back a cell along the facing (`knockBack`, KDA-257: it stays put against anything that blocks its move), without stunning or hurting it, stunned or immune or not. Both players punching each other on one tick both get shoved (player 1's first; player 2 is still drawn on its old cell, gliding out).
    - **Order:** punches come after the breaths, player 1's first. A punch that moved an enemy re-reads the enemies' tick starts, as a breath does; a shoved player is traced from where it is then drawn for the rest of the tick (known gap, rare: the part of its own move it made that tick before the shove isn't checked for catches).

## Breath contract

A breath file exports one `BreathDef` (types in `src/breath.ts`): `animal`, `shape` (`line`/`cone` with `range`, `area` with `radius`, or `self`), `cost`, `cooldown`, `duration` (informational), `maxEnergy`, `speedScale`, optional `walksOnIce` (the player moves on ice as on floor instead of sliding), optional `targetsWall(cells, wall)` (the breath acts on the wall its shape stopped at; the aim preview marks it), and `fire(hit)`. Return `false` from `fire`, before changing anything, to refuse.

`fire` gets a `BreathHit`: `origin` (the player's cell), `facing`, `cells` (the shape resolved against the maze, nearest first, never walls; walls stop it, tunnels wrap; never the origin), `wall` (the wall a line, or a cone's first cell, stopped at), `enemies` (enemies in play on `cells`, nearest first; a line or cone also lists enemies on `origin` first), `state` (read only), and the effects:

- `defeat(enemy)`: +50; the enemy waits at its spawn, out of play and not drawn, blinks for the last 1 s and is back after 5 s (a patroller restarts its route). A whole Splitter splits instead.
- `addStatus(enemy, status, seconds)`: `EnemyStatus` flags `holds`, `harmless`, `blinksBeforeEnd` (the enemy blinks for the status's last 1 s, like a respawn), and a `look`. The same `name` again refreshes to the longer time.
- `addTile(pos, effect, seconds)`: `TileEffect` flags `blocksEnemies` (every AI, phantoms too, treats it as a wall; an enemy inside leaves by the shortest way; a player or patrol waypoint inside still draws enemies to the patch's nearest edge), `ice` (never under a character; the ice grid is rebuilt once after `fire`), `opensWall` (only on a wall; grows back when it ends, pushing anyone inside back where they came from, else to the nearest open cell, never under a boss in play), and a `look`. Tiles dedupe by `name` and position. A look-only tile is a pure visual.
- `push(enemy, step, distance)`: up to `distance` cells, through tunnels, stopping before walls (not at `blocksEnemies` cells); it glides in over the last cell, so it slides on if it lands on ice. Collisions that tick start from where it lands. If the glide-in would start where the player is drawn (its cell or `lastPos`), the enemy is drawn on its new cell at once and doesn't slide, so a push never catches on its own tick.

The boss (KDA-207) is outside the contract: it isn't in `enemies`, so `defeat`, `addStatus` and `push` never reach it. The engine (`breathe`) damages it by `cost / 25` once per breath when any struck cell is under its 2×2 footprint, and then the breath counts as fired even if `fire` refused. A line or cone ends at its body: its cells there are in `cells`, cells behind it aren't. For a cone, behind means further out in any lane (offset across the facing) that met the body; cells in the lanes beside it are still reached.

`Look` is `{ color?, emoji? }`: for a tile, a fill under any dot plus an emoji on top; for an enemy, a disc behind the sprite plus a small badge. Give each status and effect a unique `name` such as `"dragon.burning"`. Test with `makeState(..., { breath })` and `MoveInput.breath: true` (`src/game.breath.test.ts`, `testBreath` in `src/test/helpers.ts`, one `src/breaths/<animal>.test.ts` per breath).

Order inside `step`: breath timers count down (expiries, regrowth, respawns), with the punch's timers (KDA-288), enemies just in play roll their guard, the player moves (setting its facing), the breath fires from the player's new cell, punches land (KDA-258), enemies' tick starts are re-read, enemies move, then collisions.

## Enemies

- **Types:** up to `MAX_ENEMIES` (8) enemies a level (KDA-263), a Splitter counting as one. Enemy ids are generated (`ENEMY_IDS`, numbered from 1; the player is `CharacterId.player`, 0), one per enemy slot, `MAX_ENEMY_SLOTS` (16) of them, so 8 Splitters with their spare slots fit. An unknown AI type throws. Sprites follow the AI type (`ENEMY_SPRITES`): random 👺, chase 👹, ambusher 🦊, patroller 🤖, phantom 👻, splitter 🦠.
- **Chase AI** scores each forward candidate by BFS distance to the player with the enemy's own cell blocked, so the no-reverse rule can't send it into a dead end. Enemies heading for the same cell in a tick share one search without the block (`ChaseDistances`, KDA-263); it gives the same choice whenever the best candidate is no farther from the target than the enemy, and only otherwise does an enemy search with its own cell blocked. Ties go to direction order; an unreachable player falls back to the first candidate. On levels with ice the distance is slide-aware (`slideDistances` in `ice.ts`); without ice (`hasIce`) it uses plain `distanceMap`. Blocked tiles: enemies move on `blockedView` (blocked cells are walls) but measure distance on `goalView`, which reopens the blocked patch containing the goal, so they head for its nearest edge.
- **Spread rule** (KDA-284, phantoms KDA-285, PM calls; `spreadChoice` in `ai.ts`): chasers, ambushers and phantoms choose without chance and enemies never block each other, so two of one AI type that once stood on one cell having come from one cell made the same moves from then on and showed as one sprite (stage 1's large map: chasers 5 and 8 left the ring's bottom exit as one on every seed; stage 2's medium map: ambushers 1 and 4 on 4 of 5). Two enemies _stack_ when they would end on the same cell coming from the same cell. A chase, ambusher or Splitter enemy (whole or a half; not the halves' placing on the split tick, see Splitter) about to step onto a cell where another enemy in play of the same AI type is (moved there earlier this tick, or standing or gliding there from before) having come from the cell it is leaving takes its next-best candidate instead: by the same scoring, ties in direction order, the first that doesn't stack, if it is at most `SPREAD_SLACK` (2) moves longer than the best. If there is none (a corridor, or every other way much longer) it **holds** that move: it stays ready and chooses again next tick, so it moves on as soon as the one ahead has, and follows a cell behind instead of stacking (PM call: the slack alone still left the stage 1 chasers stacked for up to 71 s, trailing the player down corridors with no way within 2). **A hold lasts one move's time at most** (PM call, from review): `Character.heldFor` counts the seconds it has held in a row (`game.ts` adds `dt` while `enemyMove` reports a hold, and zeroes it on every tick it doesn't hold: when it moves or stays put for another reason, and while it glides, is frozen or is out of play, so a push, knock-back or split that cuts a hold short leaves nothing to shorten the next); once `heldFor * speed` reaches 1 (`mayHold`) it takes its best way and may stack for a moment. Without the limit an enemy queued behind a frozen twin for the whole freeze, and four of a kind going round a loop of cells (a 2x2 room) each held for the next forever, which could neutralise ambushers round their aim cell; now they go round at about half speed. So twins in exactly the same state can still stack again for a few move intervals in a cramped crowd, each hold running out in turn, before they part. **A twin about to leave doesn't count** (KDA-287): enemies took their turns in slot order, so a twin in a later slot that had come from the cell an enemy stands on still counted where it stood, though it was about to move on and no stack would form; the enemy behind then stepped a cell away from the player (or held) for a move instead of following straight. Now one behind a twin that came from its cell takes its turn after that twin (`turnOrder` in `ai.ts`; otherwise slot order), so it sees where the twin ended up: moved on, and it follows; still there (it held, was boxed in, or is a phantom on the player's cell), and it keeps off as before. Counting a later twin only if it isn't ready was not enough: a ready twin can still end up staying (a hold behind a third twin, say), and the one behind would then stack on it; and whether it will stay can only be known by making its choice. Changing the order can't make twins stack: of two that would end a tick stacked at least one moved, and the later of them to move saw the other already where it ended the tick and kept off, whatever the order (but for the hold limit, as before). When every enemy still to go waits on another (two that just passed each other wait on each other, as do twins going round a loop of cells) the first in slot order on that loop goes, not one merely waiting on it. No randomness (random enemies never wait); stacking (`stacksOn`), the scoring and the shared distances are unchanged. Over 5 seeds of 100 s on stages 1 SH, 2 M and SH, 3 SH, 4 SH, 6 Easy and SH, 8 H and SH, and 10 Easy and SH, moves that differ from the crowd-free choice went 2183 → 1881, and those where the twin counted then moved on 96 → 0 (65 → 0 on stage 10 SH, where Splitter halves trail each other). Mean path distance from chasers, ambushers and Splitters to the player barely moved (stage 1 SH 6.33 → 6.25, stage 8 SH 5.73 → 5.61, stage 10 SH 5.74 → 5.71, stage 6 SH 6.66 → 6.86, the rest within 0.05), as did phantoms' straight-line distance (stage 6 SH 4.91 → 4.95, stage 10 SH 5.55 → 5.61, the rest within 0.05). **Ice slides** (PM call, from review): a slide is no choice, but a chase, ambusher or Splitter enemy whose slide would carry it onto a twin (`stacksOn`) holds instead, under the same limit, and slides on a cell behind; before, two chasers that met on stage 3 slid up an ice column as one. Different AI types still share cells freely; random and patroller enemies are left alone (random ones part by chance, patrollers keep to their routes). **Phantoms** (KDA-285) keep from stacking on phantoms the same way, on their own straight-line (Manhattan) distances through walls, with the same `SPREAD_SLACK` (2), hold and hold limit. Every other step of a phantom is as near as its best or 2 farther, so it spreads whenever another step is free, at worst stepping a cell away from the player for one move, and holds only at the map edge, next to a blocked cell, or among several twins. (A slack of 0, only as-near steps, left pairs stacked for up to 5 s at a time on stage 6.) Holds can't wait on each other round a loop while phantoms go after the players, since each waits on a cell nearer the player nearest it; going home to their own spawns they can, and the hold limit lets them pass. Before, the two phantoms on stage 6 (every difficulty) and stage 4 at Super Hard went as one for 17–85 s of each 100 s run; now never. Mean straight-line distance from phantoms to the player (5 seeds, before → after): stage 6 Easy 3.85 → 4.34, Medium 3.80 → 4.04, Hard 4.34 → 5.34, Super Hard 4.42 → 4.98, stage 4 Super Hard 4.86 → 5.07 (the second of a pair now trails or flanks instead of sitting on the first). No randomness. The shared distances (`ChaseDistances`) stay exact: they give the best candidate exactly (see Chase AI), and only when it would stack does the enemy search with its own cell blocked and spread on that, so the choice is always what that search alone would give (tested against it). Where no two of a kind would stack every move is as before (tested against the moves without the crowd). Mean path distance from chasers and ambushers to the player over seeded runs (5 seeds, before → after) barely moved: stage 1 Super Hard 6.10 → 6.25, stage 2 Medium 4.92 → 4.85, stage 2 Super Hard 5.92 → 6.17, stage 3 Super Hard 6.79 → 6.56, stage 8 Hard 5.71 → 5.94, stage 8 Super Hard 5.47 → 5.91. Longest stack in those runs went from about 30–98 s (stages 1, 2, 3 and 8 at Hard and Super Hard, and the boss stages 5 and 10 at every difficulty, where two Splitters went as one; most seeds) to none. On the boss stages the spread Splitters keep a little farther off: stage 10 at Easy 3.98 → 4.92, at Super Hard 5.04 → 5.45.
- **Ambusher** aims `AMBUSH_AHEAD` (4) cells ahead along the player's latest move (straight line, no wrap; walks back to the first open cell, else the player) and reuses chase scoring.
- **Patroller** follows a looped `route: [[x, y], ...]` by shortest path, may turn back, restarts the route on respawn; pathing is slide-aware (KDA-230).
- **Phantom** ignores walls, stays on the map, doesn't use tunnels, defaults to `PHANTOM_SPEED` (1.5), never slides, keeps from stacking on another phantom (see Spread rule).
- **Splitter** (KDA-229, with the user): `ai: "splitter"`, chases at the default enemy speed. Only `defeat` splits it (+50), through `defeatEnemy`, so every defeating breath gets it. Two halves on its cell chase at `SPLITTER_HALF_SPEED` (2.5), drawn at `HALF_SCALE` (0.65), never split again, +50 each, and don't respawn alone; when both are gone it is back whole at its spawn after 5 s with the blink. A hit on the player doesn't reset it. It owns two adjacent enemy slots (the spare is out of play with `respawnIn = GONE`, Infinity), so a split never adds an enemy or moves an index; it still counts as one enemy toward the cap. Spreading: halves go to the two open neighbours farthest from the player, never onto the player's cell or `lastPos`; in a dead end they share a cell; with no free neighbour they go to the nearest free cells further out; if the Splitter stood on either player cell they start at rest. Guarantee: a split never catches the player on the split tick or right after. **Dropping dots** (KDA-270, PM call; `rules.splitterDrops: true`, `dropDot` in `splitter.ts`): every `SPLITTER_DROP_SECONDS` (4 s, a placeholder to tune at preview) each Splitter slot in play, whole or a half, drops a dot on the cell it is leaving (its `lastPos`, on the move it makes once due). Only on bare floor (no dot there, not a wall a breath opened, which would grow back over it) with no player in play on it or gliding out of it, no live boss's footprint on it (enemies don't matter), and not the spawn of a versus player out of play that will be back (it would come back standing on the dot); otherwise it stays due and tries again on its next move. Held (frozen) or harmless Splitters don't drop (they stay due); out of play (defeated, or the spare while whole) one doesn't count down. A split and coming back whole start both slots' timers afresh. A dropped dot is an ordinary dot: it scores, fills energy and counts toward `Player.dots` in versus. Why a rule, not "every boss stage": it is level data like `regrowDots`, so a boss stage can go without it, and `parseLevel` only allows it on a level with a boss, since on a dot stage a dropping Splitter would keep adding dots to clear. With `regrowDots`: regrowth belongs to the map's dot cells, so a dot dropped on any other cell never regrows once eaten, and one dropped on a map dot's cell while that dot waits to regrow is that dot back early (the wait is dropped, and once eaten it regrows as usual). Looks: a small green splat (`BURSTS.drop`) and a soft low plop (`SfxId.dotDropped`, quieter than the dot blip); the `dotDropped` event carries the enemy index and the cell. Stage 5 has it.

- **Boss** (KDA-207, with the user; `boss.ts`): one per level at most, `GameState.boss`, not an enemy slot. 2×2 (`pos` is the top-left cell), health 12 (`BOSS_HEALTH`; a level may set its own, KDA-283: `boss.health`, a whole 1–`MAX_BOSS_HEALTH` (99), checked by `parseLevel`; a multiple of 6 takes every character the same energy), damage `cost / 25` per breath (Owl 1, Dragon/Penguin/Skunk 2, Boar 3, the Boar even with no wall in front), white flash 0.15 s per hit. Can't be frozen or pushed; `blocksEnemies` tiles block it (it leaves by the shortest way), and it treats Boar-opened walls as walls. Chases at 1.5 cells/s through space where its whole footprint fits: it heads for a position covering the player, else the one nearest the player by path (it waits at a corridor's mouth), staying put on ties. Hurts on any touch of its drawn footprint (closest approach to the square of its four cell centres within `CATCH_DISTANCE`) for `BOSS_DAMAGE`, knocking the player away from the footprint's centre; it stays where it is. At 0: gone for good, +500, one big confetti burst. **Winning** (KDA-267, with the user): a boss level is cleared the moment the boss is defeated, even if a player is hit on that tick (versus applies that tick's hits first, see Versus); dots left don't matter (`cleared` in `game.ts`). Dots there are ammunition: they fill energy and score as usual. The old "dots gone with the boss alive: energy refills 10/s" is dropped (regrowing dots replace it): `parseLevel` rejects a boss level without `rules.regrowDots` (KDA-269), so a boss level never runs out of ammunition with the boss still standing. The HUD shows the boss's health as the goal: a band along the bottom of the HUD strip (`Hud.boss`, `bossHud`), in every mode, red for the health left over a pale track, under the score and bars, so it shows wherever the boss is (off screen or in fog); the bar over its sprite stays.
    - **Breath** (KDA-268; `bossBreath.ts`, its state in `Boss.breath`; the numbers are placeholders to tune at preview): the boss fights back. Its cooldown (`every`, default `BOSS_BREATH_EVERY` 4 s, counted in ticks) starts full, so the first `every` of a stage is a grace period. Once it is over, as soon as a player in play is in line and the boss is at rest on its cell (`atRest`: its glide done, so the breath always starts from where it is drawn, whatever the level's `windUp`) it winds up, facing the nearest such player (ties to player 1). In line (`inLine`): the player's cell shares a row or column with the 2×2 footprint, within `range` cells of its edge, with nothing between but floor: walls, Boar-opened walls (walls to the boss) and `blocksEnemies` tiles block the line (a player inside an opened wall can't be seen; a `blocksEnemies` tile under the player doesn't hide it). Nobody in line: it doesn't breathe but stays ready.
        - **Wind-up** (`windUp`, default 0.9 s, up from 0.75 by PM call: on stage 5 getting out of the way from a door takes two moves, and 0.75 s left too little time to react): it holds still (makes no new move), glows red-orange (a disc behind the sprite, stronger towards the breath; the sprite itself stays opaque) and a faint red-orange tint (drawn like the aim preview, `BOSS_BREATH.warning`) covers the cells it would strike, worked out each frame; a low rumble plays. The glow and tint show while playing, paused and in the ending outro (`Effects.bossBreath`), not on end screens. Then it breathes along its facing at whoever is in line by then, so a player can step aside.
        - **The breath:** 2 cells wide (the footprint's two rows or columns), `range` long (default 6), each lane stopped by its first wall or Boar-opened wall; `blocksEnemies` tiles don't stop it (they only hide a player from it). Shown for 0.4 s as a look-only tile on its cells, plus particles and a small shake; a roar plays.
        - **Hit:** every player in play standing on a struck cell (by cell, like `opponentsStruck`), or drawn within `CATCH_DISTANCE` of one's centre, is hurt through `hurtPlayer` for `damage` (default 30), knocked away from the footprint's centre, with the usual invulnerability (an invulnerable player isn't hurt). Both players can be hit; player 1 first. A knockout follows the mode's rules (solo lost, co-op out for good, versus loses its dots and respawns). Enemies are never affected. Only the breath tick hits: the 0.4 s look is harmless.
        - **Then** it waits `every` again. It never winds up or breathes while defeated or while nobody is in play (a wind-up under way is called off; the cooldown keeps counting). Defeated mid-wind-up, it doesn't breathe (the rumble already playing finishes: the audio engine can't stop one effect, and it is under 1 s, with the defeat fanfare over it).
        - **Tuning:** per level, `boss: { breath: { every, windUp, range, damage } }`, each optional (defaults in `constants.ts`); `parseLevel` checks them (every 1–60 s, windUp 0.25–5 s, range a whole 1–30, damage a whole 1–`MAX_HEALTH`). Stage 5 uses the gentle defaults; the final boss (stage 10, KDA-283) every 2.5 s, windUp 0.8 s, range 9, damage 35, with health 18.

## Movement, input and collisions

- **Speeds:** player 4 cells/s, enemies 2 cells/s by default (applied only in `createCharacter`); levels may override `playerSpeed` and per-enemy `speed`.
- **Move progress** keeps the leftover past 1 when a character moves, so speeds that don't divide 60 Hz average out. A character that is ready but can't move waits at exactly 1. The player starts ready (`moveProgress` 1); enemies start at 0, a short grace period.
- **Input:** keys are matched by `KeyboardEvent.code`. P1: arrows, ShiftRight breath, Slash (/) punch, P/Escape pause, Space confirm. P2 (KDA-211): WASD, ShiftLeft breath, Z punch, Q pause, E confirm (punch keys KDA-258, placeholders to tune at preview); both maps are always attached, and flow reads player 2 only in a 2-player mode. Auto-repeat never presses; a missed keyup or keydown recovers on the next keydown. A direction held when play starts from a menu (character select or Retry) is ignored until pressed again (`InputState.ignoreHeld`); resuming from pause isn't affected.
- **Remapping** (KDA-215, with the user): those maps are the defaults (`DEFAULT_BINDINGS` in `bindings.ts`). Per player, each of up, down, left, right, breath, punch, pause and confirm has one key, and breath, punch, confirm and pause one pad button (punch on X by default, KDA-258); the pad's D-pad and stick stay fixed. Escape always pauses P1 and goes back, and can't be bound; nor can Ctrl, Alt, Meta, CapsLock, Fn, NumLock, ScrollLock or the IME keys (KanaMode, Lang1, Lang2, Convert, NonConvert). Binding a key in use swaps the two keys, across players too. Pad buttons swap within a player only (each player has their own pad). Breath and confirm may share a button (A by default), and so may punch and confirm (KDA-258, our call: same rule, as confirm acts only in menus and punch only in play); breath and punch never share (both act in play, so one press would do both), and pause never shares. Moving onto a button whose action can't share with it swaps the two buttons whole (confirm onto Start with A shared: breath and confirm on Start, pause on A; punch onto A: punch on A, breath and confirm on X). Stored bindings fall back per entry (a key must look like a `KeyboardEvent.code`: `/^[A-Z][A-Za-z0-9]+$/`), then all keys reset if two actions share a key, and a player's pad resets if two actions that can't share are on one button. Bindings stored before the punch (or with an invalid one) give it its default key and button where they are free, else the first free spare key (/, Z, ., ,, ;, ', X, C, ...) or button it can have (X, Y, B, RB, LB, ...), player 1 first, so a save from before the punch never resets the bindings in it (KDA-258). Unknown fields in stored bindings are kept, like the settings' own. A key or button held when bindings change presses its new action only once pressed again. **Controls screen** (title path only: Settings → Controls): one player at a time, P1 first; up/down move, left/right switch between 1P and 2P. Rows: Up, Down, Left, Right, Breath, Punch, Pause, Confirm (keys), Pad breath, Pad punch, Pad confirm, Pad pause (buttons), Reset to defaults, Back. Confirm on a row prompts "Press a key/button for …"; a key row takes a key, and a pad button there cancels (so a pad-only player can get out), unless a key came on the same tick; a pad row takes a button (any pad, in the title's shared assignment), and a key there keeps it waiting. Main keeps the first key and the first button apart, so a stray press of one kind never hides the other. Escape cancels on either. While waiting, every action press is ignored, and the captured press never acts on the menu. Every change is saved (`settings.bindings`, optional, checked with `parseBindings`) and applied at once to the keyboard, the pads and the hints. Reset to defaults drops the bindings from the settings, so they follow the defaults from then on. Back or the pause action returns to Settings on Controls. The on-screen hints name P1's (and in 2-player hints P2's) bound keys; Escape is always "Esc".
- **Tap buffer:** the newest freshly pressed direction is kept in `GameState.bufferedStep` and tried first at the next move opportunity, then the held directions (`MoveInput.held`, newest first); cleared at that opportunity either way.
- **Collisions** (KDA-231, product decision) are on drawn positions (`drawnPos`: `lastPos` to `pos` by `moveProgress` capped at 1, no frame alpha). The player is touched when an enemy comes within `CATCH_DISTANCE` (0.6; sprites are `SPRITE_SCALE` 0.8 wide) at any moment of the tick, exact and wrap-aware (`wrapDelta`). Any touch hurts (see Health), including from behind (product decision), unless the player is invulnerable. Enemies aim at the player's start-of-tick cell; a chaser on the player's logical cell aims at its `lastPos`.
- **Ice:** a separate ice grid beside the cells. A character slides (except a player whose breath has `walksOnIce`, the Penguin, to whom ice is normal floor) when it stands on ice, its latest move was one cell and the next cell that way isn't a wall (enemies don't stop a slide); it moves that way on its move turns, ignoring input and AI, and stops on normal floor or against a wall. Slides go through tunnels and eat dots. A mid-slide tap stays buffered until the first free move.

## Levels, stages and screens

- **Levels:** typed `.ts` modules exporting JSON-serializable `LevelData`. A level has two maps (KDA-264), `medium` (around 21×17) and `large` (around 29×21). Each is a `LevelVariant` with its own `map`, `parTime` and `enemies` (the full list, in the order they come in). The name, `playerSpeed`, `boss` settings and `rules` are shared, and the music goes by stage. A boss stage has a `B` on both maps or neither. `parseLevel(data, difficulty)` gives the difficulty's map with the first `ENEMY_COUNT` enemies of its list, or all of them if it lists fewer. The other `E`s are left empty: no enemy, and the cell keeps its dot. Every stage has its own two maps (stages 1 to 5 remade, KDA-273 to KDA-277); `...sameForBoth({ map, parTime, enemies })` still makes one map stand in for both (one object, checked once), for test levels. Map characters: `#` wall, `.` dot, space empty, `~` ice, `:` ice with a dot, `P` player 1 spawn, `Q` player 2 spawn (optional, at most one; without it player 2 starts on `P`; every stage has one right of `P`; it holds a dot, which `createGameState` clears only when there is a player 2, so 1-player keeps every dot), `E` enemy spawn (starts on a dot), `B` boss spawn (top-left of its footprint, a dot; the other three cells `.` or space; optional `boss: { speed, health, breath }`, see Enemies → Boss). Enemies pair with `E`s in reading order. A boss level has at most one `B`, no wrap tunnels and no ice. The map is the only source of dots. `parseLevel` checks both maps whatever the difficulty (a problem with one is marked `medium:` or `large:`, unless both are the same object), each with its whole enemy list. An enemy's checks depend only on it and its own `E`, which no difficulty changes, so every prefix a difficulty can pick passes too. It rejects: no dots, unreachable dots, an open area walled off from every spawn and dot, or one where a sliding player standing anywhere can never get back to a player spawn (KDA-245: a Boar could break in and be sealed inside), an open edge cell that wraps into a wall, wrong spawn counts, more than `MAX_ENEMIES` (8) enemies (a Splitter counts 1), bad patroller routes, speeds above `TICK_RATE`, a `parTime` that isn't positive whole tenths of a second, bad `rules` (unknown keys, fog radius below 1.5, `regrowDots.seconds` that isn't positive whole tenths, `splitterDrops` that isn't a boolean or is on without a boss, a boss level without `regrowDots`), and any reachable state from which sliding can strand the player (checked from every player spawn, for a player that slides; a Penguin walks and can always get back). `parseStage`, which `levels/index.ts` loads the stages with, also rejects an enemy spawn, or a boss footprint, within `SPAWN_GAP` (`RESPAWN_CLEAR_DISTANCE + 1`, wrap-aware) of a player spawn, on either map. Test levels parsed with `parseLevel` may put them anywhere.
- **Difficulty** (KDA-264, `difficulty.ts`): Easy, Medium, Hard, Super Hard. `MAP_SIZE`: Easy and Medium play the medium map, Hard and Super Hard the large one. `ENEMY_COUNT`: 3, 4, 6, 8. Both tables are starting values, to tune after playtesting. `stages` (in `levels/`) has every stage at every difficulty, and `levels` has each at the default. Picked on difficulty select (KDA-265, see Screens), in every mode; Medium (`DEFAULT_DIFFICULTY`, today's game) by default. `DIFFICULTY_LABELS` names them and `difficultyDescription` describes each from the tables ("3 enemies, medium maze"). Character select and every game screen carry `difficulty`, so Retry, Next round and a rematch keep it. The HUD shows its name, small: with one player between the energy meter and the health gauge, with two in the middle (in versus under the round tally). The end screens (stage clear, game over, round and match results) show it as their first line ("Difficulty Hard"). Unlocks are shared across difficulties; stars and bests are kept per difficulty (see Progress). A clear is rated against its map's `parTime`.
- **Stage 5 "Boss lair"** (KDA-277, remade at two sizes; numbers to tune at preview): a walled arena in the middle (2x2 pillars and a bar, where the boss roams, along lanes at least 3 wide, the bar splitting the middle one) inside a 1-wide corridor all round it, where the boss doesn't fit and the minions start. Doors are single cells through the arena's 1-thick wall (6 on the medium map, 8 on the large), with the corridor running straight past each: from a door a player is out of a breath in two moves, from the corridor in one, and anywhere in the arena in two, never through the boss's body. All tested by `breathEscapes` in `src/test/bossArena.ts`, which counts the boss's footprint as wall: a first version walked through it, and the side lanes 2 wide between the wall and the pillars needed 4 moves. ## Rendering

- Characters glide from `lastPos` to `pos`, lagging at most one cell behind the logic; wrap tunnels slide (drawn on both edges, clipped). The HUD strip is above the maze: score left, the health gauge right (green in solo, each player's colour in 2-player), energy meter in the middle (a mark per breath's cost, amber when ready). Breath looks come from breath data; `render.ts` has no per-breath code. The boss's breath (KDA-268) is drawn the same way: its blast is a look-only tile, its warning a tint drawn by the aim preview's code (`render.ts` draws "tints": cells plus a colour, from `aimPreviews` and `bossWarning`), and its glow a `Look` from `bossLooks`, drawn as a disc behind the boss's sprite like an enemy status's.
- **Fog:** `rules.fog.radius` (at least 1.5) is purely visual: things wholly outside the radius around the player's glide position aren't drawn, with a 1.5-cell soft edge; the HUD stays visible; wrap-aware with tunnels.
- **View** (KDA-266, for the bigger mazes to come): a maze that fits whole with cells of at least `MIN_FIT_CELL_PX` (16 CSS px) is drawn whole, as before, so desktop players and today's 17×15 stages on narrow phones and short windows see the whole maze. Otherwise cells are `MIN_CELL_PX` (20 CSS px), bigger, so a maze that has to scroll is comfortable to play; the old 8 px floor is gone. Both are in `layout.ts` and are placeholders to tune on a real phone (PM decision after the PR #70 review). With the maze scrolling, the canvas takes the room it has (less the touch controls' band, `controlsSpace`) and shows part of the maze. The maths is pure, in `camera.ts`; `render.ts` only moves the maze by it.
    - **Follow:** the view centres on the player's glide position, clamped so it never shows past the maze's edges (an axis the whole maze fits along is centred), and eases after it by time (`FOLLOW_RATE` 8 per second, about half a cell behind a walking player), not per frame. A new game or a resize starts it right on the players.
    - **Two players:** it follows the midpoint of the players in play and zooms out to keep both in view with `VIEW_MARGIN` (3 cells) round each, down to the whole maze (centred, with background round it). A player out of play is ignored; with nobody in play the view holds still.
    - **What moves:** everything in maze space (cells, ice, tile effects, the aim preview, characters and their arrows, the boss, particles, the fog's light and soft edge) is drawn through the view and skipped when outside it (wrap-aware: a character half through a tunnel shows if either half is in view). The shake still moves the maze inside the clip. The HUD strip stays put. With tunnels the view stops at the edges, so a player going through one leaves one side and the view slides across to it coming in on the other.

## Polish

Decided with the user on 2026-10-27 (KDA-212–215, KDA-246):

- **Sound** (KDA-212): all sound is synthesized with Web Audio in a chip style, with no asset files; each stage has its own generated music loop.
    - **Unlock:** audio starts on the first key press or tap (`audio.unlock()`, the browsers' autoplay rule). Effects played before that are dropped; the tune asked for starts then. A gamepad press doesn't count as a gesture in browsers, so a gamepad-only player hears nothing until a key or tap.
    - **Music:** the title tune on the title, the menus (story mode's too) and settings opened from the title; the stage's tune (`stageTrack`) while playing. Pause (settings opened from pause included) freezes the tune, and resuming carries on from the same point. From the ending outro on there is no music, so the stage clear and game over jingles play alone, and the result screens are quiet.
    - **Effects:** one per game event (`soundForEvent` in `sound.ts`): a breath sound per animal, a dot blip, a soft plop for a dot a Splitter drops (KDA-270), enemy defeated, boss hit and defeated, the boss's wind-up (a low rumble) and breath (a roar, KDA-268), hurt (a short thump, KDA-257), caught (a knockout), stunned, a punch's whiff, its thwack when it lands and a thud on the boss (KDA-258), a dull short tok when an enemy blocks it (KDA-288), stage clear (also a won versus round), game over. A respawn makes none, nor does a dot growing back (KDA-269); each sound plays at most once per tick, game over replaces the knockout that lost the game, and each punch is one sound: its first hit's if it landed (an enemy's thwack over the boss's thud), else a block's tok if an enemy blocked it (KDA-288), else its whiff (so one player's punch never silences the other's). Menus blip on cursor moves, confirm, back, and pause/resume.
    - **Volumes:** Music, Effects and Mute from settings apply live, from the pause screen too. Gain is (volume / 10)²; the music bus is lifted (`MUSIC_LEVEL` 2) since the tunes' voices are written quieter than the effects; at half the effects' level the music was too faint to hear (user feedback, 2026-10-28). A limiter between master and the speakers (`LIMITER`: −3 dBFS, 20:1, hard knee) catches the peaks this allows at full volumes, so they squash instead of clipping (PR #65 review); its automatic make-up gain lifts the whole mix about 20%.
- **Settings** (KDA-246): a Settings screen opens from the title menu (after the modes) and from the pause menu (Resume, Settings, and Quit in a story run); Back returns where it came from, a paused game untouched. Remapping is on the title path only. Rows: Music volume and Effects volume (0–10 each), Mute (master), Screen shake (Auto / On / Off), Facing guide (KDA-247), Players (KDA-262: Pass through or Bump, on the title and pause paths); KDA-214 adds Show D-pad and KDA-215 Controls.
- **Reduced motion** (KDA-213): with reduced motion requested, there is no screen shake, particles are fewer and shorter, and a punch's jab doesn't lunge (KDA-258). The Screen shake row is a choice of **Auto** (the default: follows the system, off under reduced motion and on otherwise), **On** or **Off**, stored as `screenShake` `null`, `true` or `false` (with the user, KDA-251).
- **Touch** (KDA-214; KDA-261 with the user: drag to move plus two buttons, picked over "D-pad always" and "tap punch / double-tap breathe"): touch drives player 1 only, and every menu is tappable. Dragging on the board is the default; an on-screen D-pad is off by default, with a Show D-pad toggle shown only on touch devices.
    - **Drag to move** (KDA-261, `touch.ts`): touch the board anywhere that isn't a button and drag. While the finger is down the player moves the way it dragged (stronger axis, once it is `SWIPE_DISTANCE` from where it touched down); on the lift the direction is released, so the player stops at the next cell, like letting go of a key. The direction is held (`MoveInput.held`) and pressed only when it changes, so a turn dragged just before a junction is buffered like a key tap. A flick whose movement only shows on the lift is a tap of that way (one cell). A tap on the board does nothing.
    - **Fallback** (PM call, KDA-261): while the finger stays down, the direction held before the latest turn stays held behind the new one, as a keyboard player holds the old arrow while pressing the new one, so an early turn waits for its junction while the player carries on instead of stopping it at the wall. Only one fallback is kept. Reversing the way the player goes (right, then left) drops it, so the player never bounces back. Reversing the latest turn drops that turn but keeps the fallback, matching the keys (PM call after the PR #79 review): moving right, an early up then down holds down with right behind it, as holding → and pressing ↑ then ↓ would, so the player carries on to the south junction instead of stopping (`dragHeld`). Turning back to the fallback presses it afresh. The lift lets go of both, so the player stops.
    - **Re-steering** (our call): dragging another way re-steers without lifting. The distance is measured from an anchor that keeps up with the finger while it moves on the way held and stays put while it moves any other way, so a turn or a reversal always takes `SWIPE_DISTANCE` from where the finger last went the held way, however long the drag (measuring from the touch-down point would need a long drag back to turn after a long drag), and a wobble across the held way is forgotten once the finger moves on.
    - **Fingers and other input:** one finger on the board steers at a time. Another finger takes over only once it has dragged `SWIPE_DISTANCE` (the first one's directions are let go of), so a stray tap, such as a thumb that misses a button, changes nothing (PR #79 review). When the steering finger lifts with an older finger still down, that one steers again from where it is now, holding nothing until it drags `SWIPE_DISTANCE`, so the player doesn't jump; it can also take back over by dragging. Whenever the drag is let go of (a lift, the latch, a game starting or ending), every finger still down is measured afresh from where it is, so one that drifted while resting needs a whole `SWIPE_DISTANCE` to steer (PR #79 re-review). Each finger on the board is captured (`setPointerCapture`, as on the D-pad), so a pen dragged off the board keeps its moves and lift, and a lost capture lifts it. Each finger on a button is tracked by `pointerId`, so one finger steers while another presses Punch or Breath. A direction pressed by a key, pad or the D-pad drops the drag's direction and its fallback (`PlayerInput.latch`), so the newest input wins and letting go of it stops the player; the finger then steers again once it drags `SWIPE_DISTANCE` more. A drag pressed while a key is held wins the same way (it is the newest held direction). A game starting or ending (not pause/resume) lets go of the drag too, with the same rule for a finger still down. The window losing focus or the page being hidden lets go of the drag and forgets every finger on the board, since their lifts may never come (PR #79 review).
    - **Thresholds** (`touch.ts`, `dpad.ts`): a drag steers at 24 CSS px (`SWIPE_DISTANCE`); the D-pad's dead zone is 10 px from its centre. Mouse input on the board is ignored.
    - **Punch and Breath buttons** (KDA-261, `dpad.ts`): DOM buttons like the D-pad, shown during play where touch is the main input (`isTouchFirst`: the primary pointer is coarse, `(pointer: coarse)`), or beside the D-pad when it shows; a laptop with a touch screen (a touch device by `isTouchDevice`, but a fine primary pointer) gets neither the buttons nor their band (PR #79 review). The ⏸ button and the Show D-pad setting still go by `isTouchDevice`: a touch-screen laptop may be played by touch, and both cost nothing until used. The buttons sit on the right: side by side below the maze in portrait (Punch in the corner, Breath left of it), Breath above Punch right of the maze in landscape. 72 px each. With the D-pad on, a phone in portrait narrower than 349 px stacks Breath above Punch too, so nothing overlaps the D-pad down to 320 px (PR #79 review). A button presses its action on touch and releases on lift, so each tap is one press and holding never repeats; it looks pressed while a finger is on it. Breath's turns amber while player 1 can breathe (the HUD meter's rule, `energyHud(...).ready`). Where they show (without the D-pad) the maze always leaves a band for them (`BUTTONS_SPACE`, 104 px, `controlsSpace`): below it in portrait, right of it in landscape; kept on menus too, so the maze doesn't jump when play starts. On a 390×844 or 360×740 phone, either way up, today's 17×15 stages keep their cell size with this band and with the D-pad's.
    - **⏸ button:** during play on touch devices (`isTouchDevice`: a coarse pointer, or touch points), top right, since a phone has no Esc.
    - **Menus:** one tap on an item or card puts the cursor there and confirms (`selectItem`, then a confirm press), so the end screens' confirm delay still applies; a slider's ◀ and ▶ step it; Back buttons on the menus that go back (the choice of story or stage mode, the story menu, stage, difficulty and character select); the pause screen is its Resume/Settings menu; on the controls screen a tap runs a row, and while it waits for a key or button any tap on the overlay cancels, like Escape (the prompt reads "Tap to cancel" on touch devices). Key-only hints, the controls screen's included, are hidden on phones; the capture prompt itself always shows (`(hover: none) and (pointer: coarse)`).
    - **D-pad:** DOM buttons (`dpad.ts`), not drawn on the canvas, so each control has its own pointer events and CSS places them. With it on, the maze leaves a 180 px band (`DPAD_SPACE`, `controlsSpace`) in place of the buttons' band: below it in portrait (D-pad left, Breath and Punch right), on both sides in landscape (D-pad left, Breath above Punch right). The D-pad holds a direction while touched and follows the finger round it; it shows only on touch devices with Show D-pad on, applied live. With it on, dragging on the board still steers (our call, KDA-261): it costs nothing, since a D-pad press drops the drag and a resting thumb doesn't move 24 px, and a player can use either.
- **Remapping** (KDA-215): per player, one key each for up, down, left, right, breath, punch, pause and confirm, plus pad buttons for breath, punch, confirm and pause; the pad's D-pad and stick are fixed. Escape always pauses and backs out and can't be rebound. Binding a key already in use swaps the two bindings, across P1 and P2 too. Reset to defaults restores them all. Details, including pad sharing and the controls screen, are in the Remapping bullet under "Movement, input and collisions".
- **Game events** (KDA-246): `step` reports what happened in each tick as `GameState.events` (`GameEvent` in `events.ts`: dots eaten, grown back (`dotRegrown`, KDA-269) and dropped by a Splitter (`dotDropped`, KDA-270), breaths, defeats, boss hits, the boss's wind-up and breath (`bossWindUp`, `bossBreathed`), hurts, knockouts (`playerCaught`; a knockout reports no `hurt` as well), respawns, stuns, punches, their hits and blocks (`punchBlocked`, KDA-288), win and loss), one event per occurrence. The punch's events (`punched`, `punchHit`, `punchBlocked`) give `at` as a drawn position, not a whole cell. Main takes them after each tick (`takeEvents`) and hands them to `onGameEvents`, where sound and effects hook in.
- **Particles and shake** (KDA-213; looks and amounts are placeholders to tune at preview):
    - Visual only: `particles.ts` and `shake.ts` are pure, and nothing in the game reads them. `eventEffects.ts` maps each game event to effects. A dot eaten gives crumbs; a dot growing back a faint green sparkle (KDA-269); a dot a Splitter drops a small green splat (KDA-270). A breath gives its own look (`BreathDef.particles`) on every cell its shape covers, flying along the facing. An enemy defeated gives a defeat burst and a hit shake. A boss hit or defeat gives a burst at the footprint's centre and a shake. The boss's breath gives its look (`BOSS_BREATH.particles`) on every cell it struck, flying along its facing, and a small shake (`SHAKES.bossBreath`); its wind-up gets none (its glow and tint are drawn from its state). A hurt gives a smaller red burst and a small shake (KDA-257); a knockout gives the red burst and the caught shake. A versus stun gives a small shake. A punch that lands gives a tiny burst on each thing it hit and one very small shake, however many it hit (KDA-258); a block gives neither, only its shield (KDA-288). Respawns, wins and losses get nothing.
    - **Jab look** (KDA-258, placeholders to tune at preview): for 0.15 s after each punch (`jab.ts`, started from `punched` events and moved on with the effects, so it freezes while paused) the player's sprite lunges up to 0.15 cell along the punch and back, with a small 👊 0.6 cell ahead, drawn over every character (so the enemy it hits doesn't hide it). The facing arrow stays with the glide, not the lunge. With reduced motion there is no lunge; the fist still shows. Drawn from the jab data, with no per-animal code.
    - **Shield look** (with the user, KDA-288; placeholders to tune at preview): for 0.3 s after an enemy blocks a punch (`shield.ts`, started from `punchBlocked` events and moved on with the effects, so it freezes while paused) a 🛡 shows over it, centred on its sprite wherever it is drawn and over every character, like the jab's fist.
    - The shake moves the maze inside its clip; the HUD and the fog's darkness stay still. The offset is in cells (up to `MAX_SHAKE` 0.25 cell), by trauma squared; trauma drains at 2.5 per second.
    - Effects move with the game's ticks, so they freeze while paused. A new game starts with none. Particles show during play, pause and the outro, and are hidden on end screens. They are hidden outside the fog's light.
    - Reduced motion: particles are 35% as many (at least 1), with half the life and half the speed. The shake is `shakeEnabled(settings, reduced motion)`, recomputed on load, on every settings change and when the system setting changes.
- **Facing guide: arrow (default) or aim preview, a setting** (KDA-247, with the user): Settings → Facing guide (`settings.facingGuide`, `"arrow"` or `"aim"`, default `"arrow"`; a `choice` row, ◀ ▶ cycles and wraps, on the title and pause paths). main passes it as `Effects.guide`.
    - **Arrow:** a small filled triangle in the player's `PLAYER_COLORS` colour just outside its ring (tip 0.7 cell from the sprite's centre, 0.3 cell wide), pointing along `Breather.facing` (right before the first move). Shown only while the player could breathe (the aim preview's rule: charged, in play, not stunned), in solo too (where there is no ring). Drawn with the sprite at its glide position, so it moves with it and the shake. Shown while playing, paused and in the ending outro, like the rings; not on end screens.
    - **Aim preview:** while a player's breath is ready (the meter's amber rule, `canBreathe`), it is in play and not stunned, and only on the playing screen (not paused, ending or end screens), the cells its breath would hit get a faint tint in its `PLAYER_COLORS` colour (blue P1, red P2; P1 blue in solo too) with a slightly stronger edge round the shape, drawn under the dots and characters and hidden outside the fog's light. The cells come from `aimCells` in `breath.ts`, which `breathe` shares: the shape from the player's logical cell along its facing (right before it has moved), stopped by walls and the live boss's body, wrapping through tunnels. A breath that acts on walls (`BreathDef.targetsWall`: the Boar) shows only the wall it would break, and nothing when there is none in front (open floor or an outer wall), even though its headbutt still hits the boss or stuns a versus player there (with the user, KDA-252). A breath that would reach nothing (a line facing a wall) shows nothing. Both players' previews show, overlapping. Alpha 0.1 fill, 0.3 edge (tuned at preview).
- **Settings storage** (KDA-246): `{ version: 1, musicVolume, effectsVolume, muted, screenShake, showDpad, facingGuide, playersBump }` under `dot-eater:settings:v1`; defaults 7, 8, off, unset (`null`), off, `"arrow"`, off (`false`, pass through; a save from before KDA-262 reads as off). Bad JSON, another version or a non-object gives the defaults (no migrations); an invalid field on its own falls back to its default, and unknown fields are kept. New settings (KDA-215's bindings) are added as optional fields without a version bump.
